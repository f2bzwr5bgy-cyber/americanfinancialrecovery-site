What are surplus funds?

When a county sells a property at a tax deed sale, or a court-ordered foreclosure auction is held, the winning bid sometimes exceeds the taxes, loan balance, fees and costs owed. That difference is the surplus.

$180,000Winning auction bid
−
$42,000Taxes, debts & costs owed
=
$138,000Possible surplus

Illustrative example only. Actual amounts vary and may be reduced by liens, mortgages or other claims.

In Florida, these funds are typically held by the county Clerk of Court. They may be claimed by the former owner, lienholders, or, in some cases, heirs. Claim deadlines apply, and unclaimed funds may eventually go to the county or the state. That's why acting early matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a scam?

It's smart to be careful. Surplus funds are real, and records of them are generally public. Here is how to protect yourself with anyone, including us:

  • We will never ask for money upfront.
  • We will tell you where the funds are being held so you can check with the Clerk of Court yourself.
  • You'll receive a written agreement before you sign anything.
  • You are always free to file a claim on your own instead.

Got a letter from us? See how to confirm it.

Can I file the claim myself?

Yes. Former owners can generally file a claim directly with the Clerk of Court or the court handling the case, without hiring anyone. Many people choose to work with us because the process can involve research, documentation, court filings and deadlines. The choice is always yours.

Is there a deadline?

Yes. Deadlines depend on the type of sale and the county, and they can be short. Funds not claimed in time may be kept by the county or sent to the state. Contact us as soon as possible for a free review so we can check where your case stands.

What does it cost?

Nothing upfront. You pay nothing unless funds are recovered, and the terms are explained in a written agreement before you sign anything.

I still had a mortgage or liens. Can I still get money?

Possibly. Mortgages, liens and other recorded claims may need to be paid from the surplus before the remaining balance goes to the former owner. Even with a mortgage, there may be money left over. We'll review the records and explain what we find.

The owner passed away. Can the family claim?

Often, yes. Heirs or the estate may be able to claim. This may require documents such as a death certificate, a will, or probate court paperwork. We can help you understand which documents may be needed.

How long does it take?

Timing varies by county and case. Some claims are resolved in weeks; others, especially those involving liens, multiple claimants or estates, can take several months.

How did you find me?

Tax deed and foreclosure sale records are public. We review those records to identify former owners who may be entitled to funds and reach out to let them know.
